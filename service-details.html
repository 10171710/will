Section 01 · Estate Administration

Probate assistance & court estate settlement

Probate is the court’s official confirmation that a will is valid and that the named executor has the legal authority to manage and distribute the estate. Being named an executor is an honour that often arrives during grief. Our practice takes on the procedural weight, filings, and court appearances so you can focus on family.

Required Documents

The primary documents needed to commence a probate petition:

  • Original Last Will and Testament of the deceased
  • Original certified Death Certificate
  • Full schedule of movable & immovable assets
  • Identity and address proofs of all legal heirs
  • Contact details of the two attesting witnesses
  • Title deeds, share certificates & bank passbooks

Missing a title or statement? We assist in obtaining certified duplicates from authorities.

Crucial Guidance for Executors

If you have recently been named an executor, do not distribute estate funds, close accounts, or sell property before the grant of probate is issued. Executors can be held personally liable for early distributions if subsequent claims or undisclosed debts arise.

Section 03 · Knowledge Hub

Frequently asked questions about estate planning & probate

Clear, unhurried answers to the 10 questions clients ask us most frequently.

Showing 10 questions

Honestly, how long does probate take in India?‎

For an uncontested probate petition in Karnataka, the process typically takes between 9 to 14 months from the date of filing. The timeline includes lodging the petition, public newspaper citations, filing witness affidavits, registry verification, and the formal court grant. If a caveat or formal objection is lodged, the petition converts into a contentious testamentary suit, which can take longer.

Is probate legally compulsory for every will?‎

Under the Indian Succession Act, probate is mandatory for wills executed within the historic Presidency towns (Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) or covering immovable properties situated there. In other jurisdictions like Bengaluru, it is optional unless demanded by institutional registrars, housing societies, or banks. However, obtaining probate eliminates title doubts and protects against contested claims.

What is the difference between probate and a succession certificate?‎

Probate is granted only when there is a valid written will, confirming the executor’s authority over the entire estate. A succession certificate is applied for when someone dies intestate (without a will) specifically to collect movable debts and securities (bank deposits, shares, mutual funds).

Do I really need a will if my family already agrees on my wishes?‎

Yes. Knowing your wishes and having the legal power to execute them are completely different. Banks, depositories, sub-registrars, and courts cannot act on verbal agreements. Without a valid document, your family must spend 6–14 months obtaining court heirship certificates.

Can I simply write my own will by hand on plain paper?‎

Legally, yes — a handwritten will on plain paper is valid if signed by you in the presence of two witnesses. In practice, homemade wills frequently fail due to crucial mistakes: beneficiaries signing as witnesses, lack of a residuary clause, or ambiguous property descriptions. Professional drafting prevents future litigation.

Does a will have to be registered at the sub-registrar?‎

Registration is optional under Indian law, and an unregistered will is fully valid. However, registration provides strong evidentiary presumption of genuine execution, mental capacity, and safe custody in government records, making it difficult to challenge.

My bank has a nominee named. Does the nominee become the owner?‎

No. The Supreme Court of India has established that a nominee is merely a custodian appointed to receive funds from the institution. The assets remain part of your estate and belong to the legal heirs or beneficiaries specified in your will.

What does a private trust achieve that a will cannot?‎

A will takes effect only upon death and passes through probate or succession procedures. A private trust operates continuously across generations, allowing conditional distributions (e.g., funding college at 21), protecting vulnerable family members, and bypassing probate entirely.

How do I legally nominate guardians for my minor children?‎

A testamentary guardianship clause in your will legally appoints the individuals you choose to care for your children should both parents pass away before they turn 18. Without this, custody is determined by judicial discretion in family court.
