Executor's checklist: the first thirty days

What to do immediately, what to halt, and what can safely wait a fortnight. A calm, orderly roadmap for executors navigating the fog of early bereavement.

Leena Rodrigues Senior Associate · Probate & Estate Administration
Share

Being named an executor in a loved one's Will is an honour, but in the immediate days following a death, it can feel like being handed an overwhelming administrative burden while you are still grieving.

The most important thing an executor can do in the first thirty days is slow down. Very few legal matters require instant resolution. The only urgent tasks are securing property, preserving records, and obtaining death certificates.

Days 1 to 7: Immediate Care & Securing Assets

Order 15 to 20 Original Death Certificates Every bank, insurer, registrar, demat depository, and tax department demands an official municipal death certificate. Ordering twenty copies initially saves weeks later.
Secure Physical Property & Valuables Ensure the deceased's home is locked, car keys and safe keys are collected, and post/mail is redirected. Do not allow relatives to remove furniture, jewellery, or heirlooms yet.
Freeze Credit Cards & Automated Outflows Contact credit card issuers to cancel card lines and prevent subscription charges or fraud. Maintain essential home utility payments (electricity, building maintenance).
An executor's golden rule: until you have an inventory and have reviewed the original Will with a lawyer, give nothing away and promise nothing.

Days 8 to 15: Locating the Will and Identifying Beneficiaries

Once immediate funeral ceremonies have concluded, turn your attention to locating the true Original Will:

  • Check home filing cabinets, safe deposit lockers, or contact the family advocate who drafted it.
  • Ensure you have the latest executed version (check dates and revocation clauses).
  • Carefully read the appointment clause to confirm if you are the sole executor or a joint co-executor.
  • Make certified photocopies and keep the original safe from staples, dampness, or tears.

Send a gentle, written notification to the beneficiaries named in the Will acknowledging that you are stepping into your role as executor and that you will share an inventory once compiled.

Days 16 to 30: Compiling the Asset & Liability Ledger

Before filing for probate or executing asset transmissions, you must create a comprehensive balance sheet of everything the deceased owned and owed on the date of death:

Asset / Liability Documents Needed Action for Executor
Bank Accounts & Fixed Deposits Passbooks, FD receipts, TDS certificates Request balance confirmation letter as on date of death
Mutual Funds & Equities CAMS/KFintech CAS statement, NSDL/CDSL holding statement Record current market valuations
Immovable Properties Original Sale Deeds, Khata certificates, tax receipts Inspect titles; ensure property taxes are cleared
Outstanding Debts & Loans Mortgage statements, personal loans, tax dues Estate debts must be satisfied before any beneficiary distribution

What Can Safely Wait Past 30 Days

Executors often rush to settle arguments or distribute specific items. Here is what can safely wait until months 2 through 6:

  • Selling estate real estate: Never enter sale agreements until you have probate or clear title transmission.
  • Distributing cash bequests: Creditors have first claim on the estate; early distributions make the executor personally liable if tax or debt liabilities emerge later.
  • Closing bank accounts: Maintain one primary estate account for paying ongoing estate expenses and legal fees.

Consulting an Estate Specialist

If the estate involves commercial properties, overseas beneficiaries, or substantial assets in Mumbai, Kolkata, or Chennai (where probate is mandatory under the Indian Succession Act), consult an experienced estate practitioner before submitting court petitions.
